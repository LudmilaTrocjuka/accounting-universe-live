Making Tax Digital for Income Tax: Missed the 7 November 2026 Deadline? What Sole Traders Need to Know

The second quarterly reporting deadline under Making Tax Digital (MTD) for Income Tax passed on 7 November 2026. For self-employed business owners, what happens if the update was not submitted on time?

HMRC has introduced transitional treatment of penalties for the first year. However, this does not remove the requirement to keep digital records or submit quarterly updates. For sole traders running cafés, hospitality businesses, consultancy practices and other small enterprises, the distinction matters.

1. Who must use MTD for Income Tax?

Making Tax Digital for Income Tax is being introduced in stages, according to qualifying income.

Qualifying incomeMandatory start
Over £50,000 in 2024/256 April 2026
Over £30,000 in 2025/266 April 2027
Over £20,000 in 2026/276 April 2028

Qualifying income generally means total gross income from self-employment and property before deducting expenses. For example, a self-employed café owner with £65,000 of qualifying income and £48,000 of allowable business expenses still exceeds the £50,000 threshold. Income from employment, dividends and pensions is not included in this threshold. HMRC uses Self Assessment information to establish when a taxpayer must join. Some taxpayers may qualify for exemption, including digital exclusion.

What about Limited Companies and LLPs?

MTD for Income Tax does not apply to a limited company simply because its turnover exceeds £50,000: companies have separate Corporation Tax obligations. Partnerships, including LLPs, are not currently included in the mandatory rollout timetable; HMRC will announce their timetable separately. Individuals with qualifying self-employment or property income in their own right should check their personal position.

2. What was due on 7 November 2026?

The second quarterly update for 2026/27 was due on 7 November. Standard reporting covers 6 April to 5 October 2026; alternative calendar reporting covers the period ending 30 September. The deadline is identical.

Quarterly updates summarise income and expenses drawn from digital records; they are not separate annual tax returns. Submitting an update does not create an additional Income Tax payment deadline.

The remaining quarterly deadlines for 2026/27 are 7 February 2027 and 7 May 2027. Annual filing and payment obligations remain subject to the applicable Self Assessment deadlines.

3. Will HMRC issue a penalty for missing the deadline?

For 2026/27, HMRC will not apply penalty points for late MTD Income Tax quarterly updates. Nevertheless, reporting remains mandatory, outstanding updates must be sent before submitting the annual tax return through compatible software, and the transitional arrangement does not remove penalties for late annual returns or late payments.

For later tax years, the points-based system applies to mandatory quarterly updates. Reaching four points results in a £200 penalty, with further £200 penalties for subsequent missed deadlines. Business owners should use the first year to establish reliable reporting rather than assume late submissions will remain penalty-free.

4. What should you do if your quarterly update is overdue?

Bring digital records up to date and submit the outstanding update. Review the following areas:

Check sales and income records

Confirm business income is complete and consistent. Hospitality businesses may need to reconcile EPOS reports, card processor settlements, cash takings, bank deposits and delivery-platform receipts. A net card settlement is not necessarily the same as the gross sale: fees, refunds and timing can create differences.

Review business expenses

Check supplier invoices, payroll-related costs, rent, utilities and other expenses are appropriately recorded. Not every bank payment is an allowable business expense.

Reconcile bank transactions

Compare accounting records with bank statements to identify omissions, duplicates and classification errors. Bank reconciliation is a useful financial control, not a separate MTD submission requirement.

Submit through compatible software

Check that the software supports MTD for Income Tax and is authorised to communicate with HMRC. Quarterly updates do not require accounting or tax adjustments before submission; HMRC says those can be dealt with subsequently. Send the outstanding update when the underlying digital records are complete.

5. A practical example: a self-employed café operator

The following example is illustrative and does not describe an actual Accounting Universe client.

A café operated by a sole trader receives payments through a till, card terminal and delivery platform.

ItemIllustrative amount
Gross customer sales£45,000
Supplier and operating expenses£29,000
Difference before further adjustments£16,000

The £16,000 difference is not necessarily taxable profit. Additional adjustments, allowable deductions, capital expenditure or other matters may be relevant.

Suppose a delivery platform transfers £4,700 to the bank after deducting £300 in fees from £5,000 of customer sales. Recording only £4,700 as sales could understate gross income and omit platform expenses. The correct treatment depends on the underlying arrangements, but importing bank transactions alone may not produce complete records.

Accurate sales and expense records can also improve visibility over margins, costs and cash flow.

6. Does MTD replace the annual Self Assessment tax return?

No. MTD changes how affected taxpayers maintain records and report during the year. They still submit an annual tax return with necessary adjustments and other relevant income.

For 2026/27, the annual filing and payment deadline is generally 31 January 2028. Those who joined MTD in April 2026 must also remember that their 2025/26 Self Assessment return remains due by 31 January 2027 under the previous reporting arrangements. Quarterly updates are not a final annual tax calculation.

7. Why MTD matters beyond tax compliance

Digital accounting records can give small businesses more reliable information throughout the year. A hospitality operator may spot increasing food costs, changes in gross profit margins, unreconciled card settlements, unexpected payroll expenditure or short-term cash pressure.

These issues affect profitability before annual accounts are prepared. MTD is another reason to keep regular records, but effective financial control requires more than quarterly submissions. The goal is information that supports decisions.

8. Frequently asked questions

Can I submit after 7 November 2026?

Yes. Send the outstanding update. HMRC will not apply quarterly late-submission penalties for 2026/27, but reporting remains mandatory.

Do I need to pay Income Tax every quarter?

No. Quarterly updates do not introduce quarterly Income Tax payments; existing payment rules continue.

Does MTD Income Tax apply to my limited company?

No. Company Corporation Tax obligations are separate. MTD for VAT may apply independently.

Do LLP partners need to use MTD for Income Tax?

Partnerships, including LLPs, are not yet within the mandatory rollout timetable. Partners should check whether they have separate qualifying income outside the partnership.

What if my qualifying income is below £50,000?

You may join a later phase depending on qualifying income and the relevant tax year. Check HMRC thresholds rather than profit alone.
