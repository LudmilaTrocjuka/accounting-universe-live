Package Travel Regulations 2027: what UK travel agents should review in their financial controls

The UK Package Travel and Linked Travel Arrangements (Amendment) Regulations 2026 take effect on 6 April 2027. For travel agents and tour operators, the changes can affect how bookings are classified, protected and monitored.

The new rules are particularly relevant to agencies facilitating separately selected and paid-for travel services during a single visit or contact with a point of sale. This guide focuses on practical finance and record-keeping considerations, rather than individual legal advice.

What changes in April 2027?

Under the amended rules, arrangements previously classified as Linked Travel Arrangement (LTA) Type A will be treated as packages when the statutory conditions are met. LTA Type B is removed from the framework. The amendments also introduce a 14-day supplier refund right in specified circumstances and clarify rights of redress.

The changes are not retrospective for package contracts or LTAs entered into before 6 April 2027. The precise classification of an individual booking depends on how it is offered, selected and contracted.

Why the finance function should be involved

Moving a booking into the package category may change obligations relating to financial protection and responsibility for services. Finance teams need accurate booking data and a clear understanding of which party is responsible for each transaction.

Five practical checks for travel agencies

  1. Map the booking journey. Identify when customers select more than one travel service, whether payments are separate and whether bookings happen during a single visit or contact.
  2. Document booking classification. Retain records supporting the classification and the contract date, particularly around the April 2027 transition.
  3. Review financial protection. Seek specialist advice on the relevant protection arrangements, including ATOL where flight-inclusive packages are involved.
  4. Reconcile payments and refunds. Match customer receipts, supplier payments, commissions, cancellations and refunds to the underlying booking and supplier.
  5. Forecast cash exposure. Model the timing of customer refunds and supplier recoveries, and monitor working-capital needs under realistic cancellation scenarios.

Supplier refunds: an additional control point

The amendments introduce a right to a refund from a third-party supplier within 14 days in certain cases where a service is cancelled or not performed. Agencies should maintain evidence of the booking, payments made, cancellation dates and supplier communications. The statutory conditions matter; a 14-day recovery should not automatically be assumed for every supplier dispute.

What should management do before April 2027?

Bring together operations, legal/compliance and finance teams to test representative booking scenarios. Update procedures and customer information where required, and make sure management reporting can distinguish relevant package bookings and associated liabilities.

Frequently asked questions

What changes to linked travel arrangements in April 2027?

Existing LTA Type A arrangements are brought within the definition of a package, while LTA Type B is removed. Whether a particular booking qualifies depends on its facts and the amended rules.

Will every separate flight and hotel booking become a package?

No. The classification depends on the way travel services are selected, sold and contracted, including whether they are booked during a single visit or contact with a point of sale.

What financial controls should a travel agency review?

Review booking classifications, client-money and financial-protection arrangements, supplier payment and refund records, reconciliation, cash-flow forecasts, and the audit trail for each booking.

Do the amendments apply to existing bookings?

The amendments do not apply retrospectively to package travel contracts or linked travel arrangements entered into before 6 April 2027.

Official references: SI 2026/455 and UK Government guidance. This article provides general financial-management information, not legal or regulatory advice for a specific booking.
